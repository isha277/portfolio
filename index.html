Case Study Redesign B2B

Wooden Jug Website Redesign

A UX/UI case study focused on improving the usability, information architecture, and visual design of the Wooden Jug website — with a focus on B2B customer needs.

01 — Overview

Wooden Jug is a wholesale supplier of artisanal wooden homeware products. Their existing website presented a cluttered, difficult-to-navigate experience that was failing business buyers. The goal was to redesign the site to clearly communicate the product offering and streamline the B2B purchasing journey.

My Role

UX Researcher, UI Designer — end-to-end case study

Duration

3 weeks · Self-initiated case study

Tools Used

Figma, Notion, pen & paper

Deliverables

Heuristic audit, wireframes, hi-fi UI screens

02 — Problems Identified

Before redesigning, I performed a heuristic evaluation of the existing site against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics. Key problems included:

  • No clear navigation — products were hard to find and categorise
  • Cluttered homepage with competing messages and no clear hierarchy
  • Product pages lacked structured information for B2B buyers (MOQ, lead times, pricing tiers)
  • No clear user flow from browse → enquiry → contact
  • Outdated visual design that did not reflect the brand's quality

03 — Research Insights

Based on heuristic analysis and assumed B2B buyer personas, the core insight was: business buyers need speed and clarity. They visit with a specific goal, want to find what they need quickly, and need enough information to make a purchasing decision or place an enquiry without calling.

The existing site forced users to work too hard for basic information, creating unnecessary friction.

04 — Wireframing Process

I began with low-fidelity sketches to explore layout options, prioritising a cleaner navigation structure and a focused homepage hierarchy. Key decisions at this stage:

  • Restructure navigation into clear product categories
  • Homepage: hero with single CTA, then product highlights, then trust signals
  • Product listing page with filterable grid layout
  • Streamlined product detail page with B2B-specific info sections

05 — UI Redesign Decisions

The visual direction was warm, earthy, and premium — matching the brand's handmade, natural product aesthetic. Design decisions included:

  • Warm off-white backgrounds with natural wood-toned accents
  • Serif display font for headings, clean sans-serif for body
  • Generous white space to let products breathe
  • Sticky navigation for easy browsing
  • Clear CTAs: "View Range" and "Request a Quote"

06 — Outcome

The redesign resulted in a significantly cleaner, more purposeful website experience. Key improvements:

  • Clear product categorisation — reduced browsing time
  • Improved visual hierarchy — users understand the brand within seconds
  • Structured product pages with B2B-relevant information
  • Streamlined enquiry flow — fewer steps to get in touch
  • Modern, on-brand UI that reflects product quality

07 — Key Learnings

This project reinforced the value of starting with a thorough heuristic audit before jumping into design. Understanding the why behind usability failures led to more intentional, justified design decisions. It also highlighted the importance of designing for a specific audience — B2B users have very different needs to general consumers.

Mobile App Finance

FinTrack — Personal Finance App

A mobile-first finance tracker designed to make personal budgeting clear, calm, and accessible for young adults.

Overview

Most finance apps feel either too complex or too simplistic. FinTrack aimed to find the middle ground — giving users meaningful insight into their spending without overwhelming them with data.

Problem

Young adults often avoid budgeting because existing apps feel intimidating. The challenge was designing an interface that presents financial data in a way that feels manageable and even motivating.

Process

  • User interviews with 5 participants aged 20–30
  • Competitive analysis of existing finance apps
  • Low-fi wireframes → mid-fi prototype → usability testing
  • Iterated based on 3 rounds of feedback

Solution

A clean dashboard with a monthly spending summary, category breakdown, and a simple "remaining budget" indicator. Onboarding was reduced to 3 screens. Tone of voice was calm and non-judgmental.

Outcome

  • Task completion rate improved from 60% → 91% in usability tests
  • Users described the app as "simple" and "reassuring"
  • Strong positive feedback on the spending visualisation screen
Dashboard Healthcare

HealthBridge Patient Portal

Redesigning a patient-facing healthcare portal to reduce anxiety and make medical administration feel human.

Overview

Healthcare portals are often confusing and clinical. HealthBridge needed a redesign that prioritised clarity, accessibility, and a sense of calm for patients managing their health online.

Problem

Patients struggled to find appointment details, access test results, and request repeat prescriptions. The existing portal had poor navigation, dense text, and no mobile optimisation.

Process

  • Heuristic evaluation of the existing portal
  • Accessibility audit (WCAG 2.1 AA)
  • Persona development for primary user groups
  • Full wireframe → prototype → test → iterate cycle

Solution

A redesigned dashboard with a clear overview of upcoming appointments, recent results, and a simple prescription request flow. Accessible colour contrast, large touch targets, and plain language throughout.

Outcome

  • Navigation errors reduced significantly in usability testing
  • WCAG AA compliance achieved across all primary screens
  • Patients described the experience as "much clearer" and "less stressful"
